## 📖 Reading Passage: Meritocracy and Its Discontents: Luck, Effort, and Upward Mobility (优绩主义的迷思：运气、努力与阶层流动的制度困境)

In contemporary democratic societies, few ideals enjoy such universal reverence as meritocracy—the principle that wealth, power, and social standing should be awarded strictly on the basis of individual effort and talent. On its surface, this philosophy appears incomparably fairer than the feudal aristocracies or nepotistic regimes of the past. A meritocratic order promises a level playing field where humble origins pose no insurmountable barrier to extraordinary achievement. However, prominent political philosophers and sociologists have begun interrogating the darker underbelly of this seductive doctrine, arguing that modern meritocracy has generated profound social resentment and civic fractures.

As philosopher Michael Sandel compellingly articulates, the fundamental flaw of meritocracy lies in its corrosive moral rhetoric. When those who arrive at the top believe that their success is entirely their own doing, they inevitably cultivate hubris—a smug, self-congratulatory conviction that they fully deserve their laurels. By logical extension, this mindset implies that those who struggle or are left behind must also deserve their plight due to insufficient grit or cognitive inadequacy. Unlike old feudal orders, where the poor recognized their misfortune as an accident of birth, the meritocratic ethos inflicts an agonizing psychological injury: the humiliation of personal failure.

Furthermore, the premise that modern competition is genuinely impartial is an empirical fiction. Extensive sociological investigations reveal that socioeconomic advantages are inherited and amplified across generations through elite tutoring, legacy networks, and accumulated cultural capital. Children from high-earning households are exponentially more likely to attend prestigious universities and secure lucrative corporate appointments, effectively laundering ancestral privilege through competitive academic credentials. What purports to be a fair race is heavily rigged before the starting gun is even fired, yet the winners celebrate their victory as pure personal virtue.

Healing the social fractures wrought by hyper-meritocracy requires a profound shift in civic humility. We must openly acknowledge the immense role that unearned contingency—such as genetic endowment, family stability, and favorable historical timing—plays in every human triumph. When successful individuals recognize their indebtedness to societal infrastructure and sheer good fortune, hubris gives way to solidarity. Creating a truly just society requires not merely opening ladders of upward mobility for the talented few, but dignifying the essential labor of all citizens, regardless of their academic credentials or market compensation.

### 🏮 全文逐段地道精翻

在当代民主社会中，极少有哪种理念能够像“优绩主义（Meritocracy）”那样享有如此普遍的尊崇——这一原则坚信，财富、权力和社会地位应当严格依据个人的才能与努力来进行分配。表面看来，这种哲学比起过往的封建贵族世袭或任人唯亲的裙带体制显得无比公平。优绩主义秩序承诺提供一个平等的竞技场，出身卑微不再是通往非凡成就不可逾越的鸿沟。然而，杰出的政治哲学家与社会学家已经开始审视这一诱人学说背后的阴暗面，指出当代的优绩主义正在制造深刻的社会怨怼与公民群体割裂。

正如哲学家迈克尔·桑德尔（Michael Sandel）所深刻阐释的那样，优绩主义的致命软肋在于其极具侵蚀性的道德话语。当那些跻身顶层的人深信自己的成功完全是凭借个人奋斗赚来的时候，他们不可避免地滋生了傲慢——一种沾沾自喜、自以为完全配享一切桂冠的优越感。顺理成章地，这种逻辑反向暗示了：那些陷入困境或被时代抛弃的人，其落魄下场也纯粹是由于自身毅力匮乏或智力低下所致，纯属“咎由自取”。在古老的封建秩序下，贫困者至少还能将不幸归咎于出身投胎的意外；而优绩主义的道德伦理却给挣扎者带来了撕心裂肺的心灵创伤：个人无能与失败的耻辱。

不仅如此，现代社会竞争真正公平公正的预设，本身就是一种经不起推敲的实证神话。广泛的社会学调查表明，社会经济优势正通过精英辅导、校友推荐人脉网络以及累积的文化资本在代际间被持续继承与放大。富裕家庭的孩子进入顶尖学府并斩获高薪职位的概率高出数倍，他们成功地通过激烈的学术考核文凭将祖辈特权洗白。这场号称公平的赛跑在发令枪响之前就已经被严重操控，然而胜利者却依然将冲线夺冠吹嘘为个人的纯粹美德。

弥合极端优绩主义撕裂的社会伤口，需要公民在心智上完成一次深层的谦逊转向。我们必须坦然承认那些未付代价的偶然运气——例如与生俱来的基因天赋、稳固的家庭背景以及恰逢其时的历史风口——在每一次世俗胜利中所扮演的决定性角色。当成功者真正领悟到自己对公共社会基础设施与纯粹运气的深切亏欠时，自大的傲慢才会让位于休戚与共的团结。建设一个真正正义的社会，绝不仅仅在于为少数天资过人者架设向上攀爬的阶梯，更在于让所有普通公民的辛勤劳动都获得应有的体面与尊严，无论他们持有何种文凭，市场定价几何。
