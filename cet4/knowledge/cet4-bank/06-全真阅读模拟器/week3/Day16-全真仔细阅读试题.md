## 📖 Reading Passage: Fast Fashion and the Hidden Environmental Toll of Disposable Apparel (快时尚与一次性服饰的隐形环境代价)

For past generations, acquiring clothing was a deliberate, durable investment. Garments were carefully chosen, routinely mended, and worn across many years until completely unwearable. Over the past two decades, however, global fashion conglomerates have fundamentally revolutionized consumer culture by orchestrating the rise of 'fast fashion.' By compressing design-to-retail production cycles from several months to a mere few days, brands now churn out as many as fifty-two discrete micro-seasons per year. Inexpensive garments are designed not for longevity, but for rapid obsolescence—encouraging shoppers to treat clothing as disposable single-use items.

The ecological consequences of this hyper-accelerated consumption are staggering. The textile industry currently ranks among the most resource-intensive industrial sectors on Earth, generating approximately ten percent of all global carbon emissions and consuming colossal volumes of fresh water for crop irrigation and chemical dyeing. Furthermore, more than sixty percent of contemporary fast-fashion apparel is manufactured from petroleum-derived synthetic fibers such as polyester and nylon. When these synthetic garments are laundered in domestic washing machines, they shed trillions of microscopic plastic fibers that evade municipal filtration systems, ultimately contaminating marine food webs and drinking water reservoirs.

Downstream, the disposal of discarded garments presents an insurmountable waste-management crisis. The average consumer now wears an item of clothing an estimated seven to ten times before discarding it. Billions of discarded garments, often shipped under the guise of charitable donations to developing nations in the Global South, end up overwhelming local municipal landfills or washing into coastal waters, choking local ecosystems and stifling indigenous textile economies. The illusion of cheap fashion is sustained only by externalizing catastrophic ecological damage onto marginalized communities.

Curbing this environmental catastrophe requires an aggressive paradigm shift toward circular fashion. Governments must enforce Extended Producer Responsibility laws, compelling apparel corporations to fund recycling infrastructure and take financial ownership of their products' end-of-life disposal. Simultaneously, consumers must reject the relentless psychological lure of disposable novelty. By embracing garment repair, patronizing second-hand clothing networks, and investing in high-quality, ethically produced staples, we can transform fashion from an ecological hazard into a sustainable culture of mindful craftsmanship.

### 🏮 全文逐段地道精翻

在过去的世代中，购置一件衣物是一笔深思熟虑且讲求经久耐用的投资。衣服被精挑细选、反复缝补修缮，并穿戴数年之久，直至彻底无法修补。然而在过去的二十年间，跨国时尚巨头通过策划“快时尚”的兴起，彻底重塑了大众消费文化。通过将服装从设计到上市的生产周期从数月压缩到短短几天，各大品牌如今每年能炮制出多达52个互不相同的微型潮流季。低廉的成衣在设计之初就不是为了耐穿，而是为了快速淘汰过时——诱导消费者将衣物视作一次性消耗品随意丢弃。

这种超速消费所带来的生态后果令人触目惊心。纺织业目前位列地球上资源消耗最密集的工业部门之一，产生了全球约10%的碳排放量，并在农作物灌溉与化学印染中吞噬了海量的淡水资源。更严重的是，当今快时尚服饰有超过60%由涤纶、锦纶等石化合成纤维制成。当这些化纤衣物在家用洗衣机中清洗时，会脱落数以万亿计的微塑料纤维；它们穿透市政污水过滤设施，最终大面积污染海洋生物链和人类饮用水源地。

在产业链下游，海量被抛弃的旧衣物引发了一场难以收拾的垃圾处理危机。据估算，普通消费者如今一件衣服穿过7到10次后便会将其丢弃。数以十亿计的废旧衣物往往假借慈善捐赠之名被打包运往全球南方的欠发达国家，最终彻底瘫痪当地的市政垃圾填埋场，或是随暴雨冲入沿海海域，窒息海洋生态并彻底扼杀当地本土的纺织经济。廉价时尚的华丽幻象，纯粹是建立在将灾难性的生态恶果转嫁给边缘弱势群体的代价之上。

遏制这场生态环境浩劫，迫切需要全行业向“循环时尚”进行大刀阔斧的范式转移。各国政府必须出台强制性的“生产者延伸责任（EPR）”法案，责令服装巨头出资筹建回收网络，并对其产品的最终回收处置承担直接财务责任。与此同时，消费者必须抵制一次性虚荣带来的消费心理诱惑。通过主动学习缝补修复、光顾二手闲置流转，并投资于高品质、注重劳工伦理的经典基本款，我们才能将时尚从生态公害救赎为讲求克制与敬畏的工匠文明。
