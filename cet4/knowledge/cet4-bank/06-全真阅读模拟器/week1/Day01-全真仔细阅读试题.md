## 📖 Reading Passage: The Paradox of Smart Assistants: Automated Convenience vs. Human Agency (智能助手的悖论)

From the music playlists recommended on our commutes to the shortest routes calculated on our map applications, algorithmic assistants have seamlessly woven themselves into the fabric of modern life. By predicting our preferences and automating routine choices, these intelligent systems promise unprecedented convenience, relieving us of the burden of dozens of micro-decisions every single day. In an increasingly fast-paced society, delegating trivial tasks to reliable software appears not only reasonable, but entirely necessary for preserving mental energy.

However, behavioral scientists have begun to voice profound concerns about the hidden cost of this digital convenience. When decision-making instincts are continually outsourced to machines, human cognitive abilities may subtly diminish over time. Just as physical muscles atrophy without regular exercise, our capacity for critical judgment, spatial awareness, and independent problem-solving weakens when we passively accept machine-generated suggestions. Instead of evaluating alternative options, users tend to follow algorithmic prompts with unquestioning confidence.

This subtle shift in human behavior extends far beyond minor consumer choices. A recent empirical study conducted across several metropolitan areas revealed a curious pattern among daily commuters. Participants who strictly followed automated navigation systems reported significantly fewer memorable impressions of their surroundings and experienced a noticeable decline in their geographical orientation skills. By contrast, individuals who occasionally navigated independently or took unfamiliar detours demonstrated higher levels of creative problem-solving and greater adaptability when encountering unexpected road closures. The algorithmic efficiency had inadvertently eliminated the valuable friction that stimulates human curiosity.

None of this suggests that we should abandon smart technologies and return to an analog lifestyle. Rather, the challenge lies in cultivating conscious agency in an automated age. We must recognize that algorithms are designed primarily to maximize predictability and engagement, not necessarily personal growth or cognitive resilience. To maintain our intellectual autonomy, we should intentionally preserve areas where human intuition and spontaneous exploration take priority. True technological literacy means knowing not just how to deploy intelligent tools, but when to turn them off.
