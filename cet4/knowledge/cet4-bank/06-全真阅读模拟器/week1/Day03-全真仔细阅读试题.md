## 📖 Reading Passage: Blue Light, Circadian Rhythms, and the Modern Sleep Deficit (屏幕蓝光、生物钟与现代人的睡眠赤字危机)

For millions of urban dwellers, the final visual experience of the day is not the calming darkness of a bedroom, but the vibrant glow of a smartphone screen. Whether catching up on instant messages, browsing short video feeds, or reading late-night news, this bedtime routine has become nearly universal. Yet, sleep physiologists warn that this seemingly harmless indulgence exerts a profound disruptive effect on the intricate biological clocks that govern human vitality.

At the heart of the issue is the specific wavelength of light emitted by modern digital displays. Unlike the warm amber tones of sunset or incandescent bulbs, electronic screens emit concentrated blue light, which closely mimics midday sunlight. When specialized photoreceptors in our retinas detect this frequency, they signal the brain's pineal gland to halt the production of melatonin—the essential hormone responsible for inducing drowsiness and facilitating cell repair. Consequently, the brain remains in a hyper-alert state, misinterpreting the midnight glow as broad daylight.

The consequences of chronic sleep deprivation extend far beyond morning lethargy. Clinical trials demonstrate that prolonged circadian disruption impairs the brain's glymphatic system, which actively flushes out metabolic waste and toxic proteins during deep rest. Without adequate restorative sleep, individuals experience measurable deficits in short-term memory consolidation, emotional resilience, and immune defense. Over months and years, irregular sleep cycles significantly increase the vulnerability to cardiovascular disorders and metabolic syndromes.

Restoring healthy circadian alignment does not require a drastic retreat from modern technology, but rather consistent behavioral boundaries. Health experts advocate establishing a digital sunset at least sixty minutes before sleep, replacing luminous screens with physical books or relaxing audio. Furthermore, exposing oneself to natural morning sunlight helps synchronize internal clocks and anchors daytime alertness. In a culture that frequently glorifies midnight toil, reclaiming restful sleep is perhaps the most fundamental act of preventive healthcare.
