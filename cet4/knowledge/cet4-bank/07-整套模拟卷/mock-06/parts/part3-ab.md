**The retreating night**

**A)** Elias Novak has moved a telescope twice in one career, and both moves were arguments with a streetlight. As a student he worked from an observatory forty minutes from his home town, a building that had mapped the sky since 1912. By his second year the faint galaxies he had come for had dissolved into a gray lid, and the old refractor was left to public demonstrations. He now stands on a ridge in the Atacama desert, nine thousand kilometers from a city of any size. A widely quoted survey put the price of all this plainly: eight in ten Americans have never seen our own galaxy.

**B)** Artificial light behaves unlike the other poisons. A chemical stays where it falls, and noise dies within a mile, but a lamp aimed at the pavement sends part of its beam sideways and upward, where molecules of air bend it back down. The result is a lid of scattered brightness that thickens with distance: from far away a city looks like a stain on the horizon, while its glow spreads over half the sky overhead. The lamps have also changed color, and cheap white lighting built on blue emitters wastes more of what it makes than the amber glow it replaced.

**C)** Nobody used to know how much of the planet had lost its night. Satellites have answered imperfectly but honestly: instruments on polar platforms photograph the glow leaking downward, and in 2023 European researchers published an atlas of artificial sky brightness that combines those photographs with thousands of readings taken by volunteers standing in their own gardens. The result is some thirty times finer than anything drawn before. A companion paper, revisiting the same sites over sixteen years, found the natural night brightening at close to ten percent annually.

**D)** The retreat of research is now a story of relocation. Several domes that built modern cosmology can no longer pursue faint targets. At two celebrated California mountains the sky has become many times brighter than when their telescopes were new, and the instruments are kept for teaching and for bright objects. Funding therefore follows what darkness remains. The great projects of this decade are being cut into the high desert of Chile, into a Namib reserve whose local law protects its night, onto the Antarctic ice, and eventually onto the far side of the Moon, which has no air and no radio at all.

**E)** Protection is older than most people assume. Arizona wrote the first American outdoor lighting code in 1972, after astronomers near Tucson complained, and Flagstaff, which hosts a research observatory, has required shielded downward lamps ever since. Europe arrived later and broader: French rules cap the color temperature of lamps outdoors, require shop windows and empty offices to go dark in the small hours, and tighten both limits near coasts and reserves, while Italy has restricted upward light by national standard since 2000. Officials present all of this as an energy rule rather than as nostalgia.

**F)** Darkness is not decoration but a condition of a great deal of biology, and two groups show the cost. Insects are the starkest case: a moth that circles a lamp until dawn has usually given up searching for a mate, and field counts suggest that enormous numbers of the creatures gathered at a streetlight perish there from exhaustion, burns or hungry birds. Migrating birds demand a different remedy, because many travel by night and steer by the horizon, so a blazing tower pulls them into circling until they meet glass. Several North American cities now ask for dark windows during autumn passages.

**G)** The economics are the least contested part of the argument. Much outdoor illumination never reaches the ground it was bought for: it slips past the fixture, climbs over the horizon, or shines on an empty road after midnight. Estimates of that wasted share run into the billions of dollars a year in Europe and North America alone, with the carbon that produced it rising as well. Light is therefore strange in the ledger of pollution, since it is the only contaminant that is also a purchase order, and every escaped beam is cash converted into nothing.

**H)** The lighting industry rejects that framing. Its case is that efficient lamps have cut the power burned outdoors even while the lit area grew, that bright streets are safer streets, and that manufacturers now sell fixtures which dim on a schedule plus warm-colored lamps certified for astronomers. Researchers who test the first claim answer with an old observation about efficiency: when a thing gets cheaper, more of it gets used. Measurements from 2012 to 2023 show the planet's lit area expanding while each unit of light turned cheaper and bluer.

**I)** What disappears is not only data. Every society that named the stars built from them a calendar, a way of finding home and a weight of stories, and the sky of the textbooks is one region's list; many names recorded by indigenous observers belong to objects their descendants can no longer locate. Physicists worry about something plainer. A science that asks people to trust what they cannot touch has always been sold by the night overhead, and for a public that never looked up, the claim is now an abstraction.

**J)** The remedy is cheap and fits in four instructions. Fit a hood to the lamp so that nothing points above the horizontal. Aim it down and use the least light that does the job. Choose a warm tone, keeping the blue end of the spectrum for places that must show color. Put the whole system on a timer or a sensor, since a road with no traffic at three need not be lit for it. Towns that have done these things report that residents stop minding the dark and begin to notice the stars.

**K)** No map returns the sky Novak grew up under, and the glow keeps spreading. Yet the map has changed sides. Darkness used to be something an astronomer traveled to; it is now cataloged and argued for in the language usually saved for wetlands and old forests, and a town with a black horizon owns something worth selling. Whether that language will be enough is a question duller than wonder: it depends on what a council decides when it replaces four thousand streetlights under one contract.

#### Section A 选词填空（Q26–35）

**词库词性分类**
- 形容词（4）：F) hourly　H) invisible　K) ordinary　O) wider
- 副词（3）：E) elsewhere　L) politely　M) rarely
- 名词（4）：A) appliances　B) circumstances　D) connections　N) statistics
- 动词（4）：C) confirm　G) ignoring　I) leaned　J) measured
- 答案 10 题（形3/副2/名2/动3）；干扰 5 词：A) appliances、B) circumstances、E) elsewhere、G) ignoring、K) ordinary

**切题说明**：本篇写的是**线上预约制对无网家庭与钟点工的排斥**（公共服务获取权／数字排斥），落点是"谁拿不到号"；与 mock-03 仔细阅读《排队与等待的公平感》（排队心理学实验：插队引发的主观不公平感、"分钟数 vs 规则"）是**完全不同切面**——彼处主体是"站在实体队伍里的人如何评价等待是否公道"，此处主体是"实体队伍消失后拿不到入口的人"，全篇不含任何心理实验、被插队情绪或公平感测量要素。

**26. H) invisible**　语法依据：`the line has become ___26___`，become 后需表语形容词；冒号后 `it runs through a booking page` 说明队伍不再占据街道，故取"看不见的"。干扰 K) ordinary（语法可立，但与"从纸质号码牌变成午夜网页"这一变化方向矛盾）；O) wider（"变得更宽"与下文"看不见"的论证线相反，且已被 33 的 distance 框架锁定）。

**27. D) connections**　语法依据：`the fastest ___27___`，the + 最高级后需名词。判定证据：同句 `since a slow page loses the queue` 把所指锁定为**网络链路的快慢**（页面加载速度决定抢号成败），与下段 broadband、data limit 同一语义场。干扰 A) appliances（"电器"不能"让页面慢慢加载"，抢号胜负不取决于家电多寡）；B) circumstances（the fastest circumstances 无此搭配）。

**28. L) politely**　语法依据：`walk-in visitors were ___28___ refused`，被动结构中被拒方式前需副词；与"没人被告知被禁，只被告知页面说了不行"的克制语气呼应。干扰 E) elsewhere（地点副词，不能修饰 refused）；M) rarely（"很少被拒"与全篇"被排除在外"的论点正相反）。

**29. F) hourly**　语法依据：`___29___ workers learn that...`，句首需定语形容词修饰 workers；`costs a day's pay, since offices reject a changed roster` 要求"按小时计酬、停工即损失、班表还会临时变动"的语义，hourly workers 是此类劳动者的标准说法。干扰 K) ordinary（"普通工人"无法解释为何一个周二上午的号恰好等于一天工资）。

**30. I) leaned**　语法依据：`who once ___30___ on a neighbor to fetch a ticket`，once 提示一般过去时，且需与 on 构成 lean on sb to do sth（指望某人替自己做某事）。词库四个动词中仅 leaned 可与 on 搭配：J) measured（及物，✗ measured on a neighbor）；C) confirm（原形）；G) ignoring（-ing）。

**31. M) rarely**　语法依据：`such a neighbor is ___31___ free at midnight`，is 与表语 free 之间需频度副词；语义上"既能跑腿又能破机器人验证的邻居恰好在午夜有空"概率极低，正合本段"被排除"论点。干扰 E) elsewhere（✗ is elsewhere free）；L) politely（politely free 语义不立，且已被 28 的方式状语框架占用）。

**32. C) confirm**　语法依据：`cannot ___32___ their own appointment`，情态动词后需**动词原形并带宾语**，词库唯一原形动词。语义：医院为无法自行在系统里确认／锁定号源的患者保留一个柜台。干扰 I) leaned、J) measured（过去式／过去分词，形式不合）；G) ignoring（-ing 不能跟在 cannot 后）。

**33. O) wider**　语法依据：`the distance ... grows ___33___ every year`，grow 作系动词后接形容词比较级，主语 distance 要求"差距变大"。词库三个形容词中只有 wider 可与 distance 构成比较关系（invisible、ordinary 均无"随年份递增"的程度含义；hourly 已被 29 的计酬框架锁定）。

**34. N) statistics**　语法依据：`nobody records it in the ___34___`，record + in the 后需可数名词复数表"统计数字"。干扰 B) circumstances（"记录进情形"搭配不立，record 的落点必须是可量化的数字口径，且下句"官方称门向所有人敞开"构成数字与现实的对照）。

**35. J) measured**　语法依据：`what is hidden is never ___35___`，is + never + 过去分词构成被动，与上文 `nobody records it` 语义闭环：看不见的队无人测量。干扰 G) ignoring（主动式 -ing，被动槽需 ignored；✗ is never ignoring）。
