**A second life for the handbag**

**A)** Two decades ago, a used designer handbag was a matter for a small classified ad and a nervous meeting in a parking lot. Today it is a logistics problem. At a distribution center outside Philadelphia, workers in cotton gloves open some four thousand parcels a day, photograph each item against a plain gray background and publish it within hours. The bag that once sat for a month now finds a buyer in two days. Consultants put the secondary trade in luxury goods near fifty billion dollars a year and rising three times as fast as primary sales, with a projected hundred billion before the decade closes. For the first time, the afterlife of an expensive object is a bigger business than the object itself.

**B)** Almost nothing on these sites is owned by the site. Most sellers consign: they ship an item, the platform prices it, stores it, photographs it, lists it and pays the seller a share, sometimes a fifth of the final sum, sometimes close to a half. Prices are proposed by software that reads every competing listing, so the same model in a different condition can carry a different number on the same screen. Revenue arrives twice, from the seller and from the buyer, which means the company's real interest is turnover rather than the profit on any single bag.

**C)** The industry exists because of a problem economists described seventy years ago. When a buyer cannot tell a genuine piece from a skillful copy, the price both can command falls toward the price of the copy, and honest goods drift out of the market. Counterfeiting has never been cheaper: workshop-grade goods now reach buyers who believe they have bought an heirloom. The platforms therefore sell neither the bag nor the storage but something scarcer, a credible claim that the object is what its label says.

**D)** That claim is expensive to make. A trained authenticator handles perhaps two hundred and fifty items a day, reading stitching, hardware, the depth of a stamp, the pattern of glue, and every judgment carries some legal risk. Machines assist with photographs and serial formats, but such models are trained on fakes that have already been caught, so a genuinely new copy is exactly what they miss. This is why the charge that looks outrageous to a first-time seller, often a third of the price, sits closer to the cost of the work than to a margin.

**E)** Two consequences of this business matter more than any fashion story. First, the sites hold the only long records of what an expensive object can be resold for, because a maker rarely sees a piece again after the boutique. Second, price behaves less like depreciation and more like scarcity. Once a model is retired, its curve bends upward, and something withdrawn in 2018 may fetch more than it cost new. Buyers who treat these goods as stores of value are, in effect, trusting a spreadsheet that a resale company maintains.

**F)** Supply is not the constraint. Comfortable households sit on large inventories of idle goods, a bag worn four times, a watch in a drawer, capital that earns nothing and occupies space. A platform converts it into cash without a garage sale and without negotiation. Regular sellers refresh a wardrobe, but the surges come when incomes tighten. During the two downturns of the past twenty years, listings climbed sharply just as fewer people paid retail, because a used designer object is one of the few assets a household can turn into money without a broker.

**G)** Sustainability is the most useful thing the industry has to say, and its economics are genuine. A pre-owned piece demands no new leather, no new hardware and no shipment of raw materials, and the argument has converted a stigma into a virtue. Younger buyers describe resale as clever rather than second-best, and they pay closer to retail when an item arrives with a documented history. The claim is not free, however. Goods travel from seller to warehouse to buyer and, in about a fifth of cases, back again; inspection, photography, packaging and returns consume part of the saving that the second life was meant to create.

**H)** The great houses have not known what to make of any of this. Resale undercuts two things they sell: scarcity and the feeling of owning something first. Some have complained that unauthorized sites trade on their names, and several have sued, usually over a counterfeit that passed through a warehouse, a case that also, awkwardly, advertised the site's role as a market. Others have gone the other way, launching buy-back programs, opening certified pre-owned sections of their own, and quietly consulting resale data before setting a price.

**I)** The deeper logic is status. A luxury object is valuable because not everyone can have it, and a resale market hands it to more people for less. That tension resolves in an odd way. Once the secondhand price is public and firm, it becomes the strongest defense of the firsthand price: if a bag reliably returns eighty percent of what it cost, the purchase stops being spending and starts being something closer to lending. Advertisers now speak of investment pieces rather than of leather, and the numbers behind that claim come from a market the brands do not control.

**J)** The business remains fragile. Handbags and a few watch models carry most of the volume, so a change in taste is a change in revenue. Winning a customer costs a great deal, the goods are expensive to move and to store, and the profit on anything ordinary is thin. Watches are simpler to inspect than clothing, clothing is returned more than handbags, and every category keeps its own arithmetic. Meanwhile the copies improve each year, and a reputation survives only a limited number of mistakes.

**K)** What may finally settle the market is paperwork rather than fashion. Regulators in Europe are moving toward a digital identity for physical goods, a record that travels with a bag and can be read by anyone. If that arrives, the cost of proving authenticity falls steeply, and with it the fee structure built on human inspection. Platforms that priced themselves as experts would then compete on something far less defensible: convenience, price and taste.

#### Section A 选词填空（Q26–35）

**词库词性分类**
- 名词（4）：I) shock　L) trace　M) transcription　F) relief
- 形容词（4）：C) modest　G) precise　D) concise　N) verbal
- 副词（3）：A) equally　O) verbatim（形/副同形，33 取副词用法）　K) respectively
- 动词（4）：B) ignored　E) emerged　J) paraphrase　H) transcribe
- 答案 10 题（名3/形2/副2/动3）；干扰 5 词：D) concise、F) relief、H) transcribe、K) respectively、N) verbal

**词库设计说明**：三组易混对句内互锁——`verbatim / verbal / respectively`（副词槽 27、33 只收副词，verbal 为纯形容词、respectively 语义不合）、`transcription / transcribe`（31 需纯名词、35 需动词原形）、`modest / concise / precise`（34 表"幅度小"只可 modest，30 表"确切含义"只可 precise）。全卷美式拼写（gray、programs、labeled），题干文本中不出现任何词库原形词（transcript 一类同根词亦已避开，防泄答案）。

**26. I) shock**　语法依据：`came as such a ___`，such a 后需可数名词单数，构成 come as a shock 固定框架。判定证据：下句"她把讲座一字不漏记下来却几乎什么都没懂"，说明考试结果是坏消息。干扰 F) relief（语法可立，但"如释重负"与"完全没听懂"矛盾）；L) trace、M) transcription（不能进入"come as a + 情绪名词"框架）。

**27. A) equally**　语法依据：`scored ___ well`，副词修饰原级副词 well，表两组"一样好"。判定证据：句子主语为 the two groups，比较双方齐备。干扰 O) verbatim（✗ scored verbatim well，词义与"程度"无关）；K) respectively（"分别地"要求逐项对应两组不同结果，而本句说两组同分，语义相反）。

**28. E) emerged**　语法依据：`a consistent pattern ___:`，主语为单一抽象名词 pattern，其后无宾语，需不及物动词过去式表"出现、显现"，冒号后即为显现的内容。干扰 B) ignored（及物，需宾语或被动语态，✗ a pattern ignored 无助动词）；J) paraphrase、H) transcribe（动词原形与第三人称单数主语、过去时间框架均不合）。

**29. B) ignored**　语法依据：`the finding has been ___ by universities`，has been + 过去分词构成被动，by 短语给出施动者。语义：各大学照样给学生配电脑，即"置之不理"。干扰 E) emerged（不及物，无被动，✗ has been emerged by）；H) transcribe（需过去分词 transcribed，形式不合）。

**30. G) precise**　语法依据：`deciding the ___ meaning of what was just said`，定冠词 the 与名词 meaning 之间需定语形容词；the precise meaning（确切含义）为固定搭配，与下文"compress, choose and rephrase"的判断任务呼应。干扰 D) concise（concise 只描述"表达简短"，不能修饰 meaning，✗ the concise meaning）；C) modest（"适度的含义"语义不立）；N) verbal（"口头的含义"与"打字/手写"语境无关且不成搭配）。

**31. M) transcription**　语法依据：`is replaced by pure ___`，介词 by 后需名词，pure + 抽象不可数名词表"沦为纯粹的抄写"。判定证据：本段主题是打字"手指跟得上说话"，因此记录变成逐字抄写。干扰 L) trace（pure trace 不成立，且 trace 已被 32 的 leave no trace 锁定）；I) shock、F) relief（"纯粹的震惊/宽慰"与记录方式无关）。

**32. L) trace**　语法依据：`leaving no ___ behind`，leave no trace behind（不留痕迹）为固定搭配，no 后需可数名词单数。语义：文字"穿过手指"却不进入记忆。干扰 I) shock（leave no shock behind 不成搭配）；F) relief（同上）；M) transcription（不可数，no 后不立且语义重复 31）。

**33. O) verbatim**　语法依据：`capture a lecture almost ___`，almost 后需副词作方式状语修饰 capture，almost verbatim（几乎逐字地）是固定搭配。干扰 A) equally（"几乎同样地"缺比较对象，且 27 已用 equally 表达"两组同分"）；K) respectively（✗ 逐项对应义）；N) verbal（形容词，不能作方式状语）。

**34. C) modest**　语法依据：`the advantage of the pen is ___:`，is 后需表语形容词，冒号后"a few points on a hard test, not a different kind of mind"给出"幅度小"的解释。干扰 G) precise（"优势是精确的"与冒号后的"几分而已"无逻辑联系，且 precise 已被 30 的 the precise meaning 锁定）；D) concise（concise 修饰言说文本，不修饰 advantage）；N) verbal（语义不立）。

**35. J) paraphrase**　语法依据：`learn to ___ whatever you intend to keep`，to 后需动词原形且带宾语从句，语义须与"用自己的话压缩"一致。干扰 H) transcribe（及物动词原形、语法可立，但全文批评的正是"逐字抄写"，与前句 type less than you hear 自相矛盾，构成近义反义陷阱）。

#### Section B 信息匹配（Q36–45）

> 本篇 11 段（A–K）、10 题：**E 段对应 2 题**（39、40）；**B、K 两段为强干扰段**（B 亦谈收费与利润、K 亦谈验证成本下降，极易被误认为 38 的出处，但无陈述句与之对应）；其余 8 段各对应 1 题。所有陈述句均与原段逐词比对实测：与原篇最长连续重合仅 2 词（远低于 6 词红线），平均句长 14.0 词（16/16/15/16/14/11/14/13/14/11）。

**36. A)**　出处：`Consultants put the secondary trade in luxury goods near fifty billion dollars a year and rising three times as fast as primary sales`
改写点：the secondary trade in luxury goods→the pre-owned luxury market；rising three times as fast as primary sales→grows several times faster than the trade in new goods。

**37. C)**　出处：`The platforms therefore sell neither the bag nor the storage but something scarcer, a credible claim that the object is what its label says`
改写点：a credible claim that the object is what its label says→a promise that an item is real；sell neither the bag→sell is not a bag。

**38. D)**　出处：`the charge that looks outrageous to a first-time seller, often a third of the price, sits closer to the cost of the work than to a margin`
改写点：sits closer to the cost of the work→close to what the checking costs；looks outrageous→seems outrageous（原句该表达前后主句框架被拆重排，未成 6 词连续重合）。D 段专讲"收费=人力成本"，B 段只讲分成比例，故唯一。

**39. E)**　出处：`the sites hold the only long records of what an expensive object can be resold for, because a maker rarely sees a piece again after the boutique`
改写点：hold the only long records→only the resale sites know；what an expensive object can be resold for→what makers' own goods are worth；after the boutique→once they leave the shop。

**40. E)**　出处：`Once a model is retired, its curve bends upward, and something withdrawn in 2018 may fetch more than it cost new`
改写点：a model is retired/withdrawn→something taken out of production；its curve bends upward, may fetch more→can later be worth more；than it cost new→than its original price。与 39 同落 E 段（E 段含"数据"与"稀缺涨价"两条独立信息），即"一段双题"。

**41. F)**　出处：`the surges come when incomes tighten. During the two downturns of the past twenty years, listings climbed sharply just as fewer people paid retail`
改写点：the surges come when incomes tighten / the two downturns→harder financial times；listings climbed sharply→send more goods to these marketplaces, not fewer；one of the few assets a household can turn into money→these marketplaces（个人资产被迫变现＝平台货源增加）。

**42. G)**　出处：`Sustainability is the most useful thing the industry has to say`；`inspection, photography, packaging and returns consume part of the saving that the second life was meant to create`
改写点：most useful thing the industry has to say→helps the platforms；consume part of the saving→moving goods around eats into its gains。

**43. H)**　出处：`several have sued, usually over a counterfeit that passed through a warehouse, a case that also, awkwardly, advertised the site's role as a market`
改写点：a case that also advertised the site's role→have backfired in one way；several houses have sued→lawsuits brought by luxury makers。

**44. I)**　出处：`Once the secondhand price is public and firm, it becomes the strongest defense of the firsthand price`
改写点：public and firm→firm；the strongest defense of the firsthand price→the best excuse for a rising retail price。注意与 E 段的"retired 涨价"区分：I 段的落点是"新品定价因此更贵"，E 段无此信息。

**45. J)**　出处：`Handbags and a few watch models carry most of the volume, so a change in taste is a change in revenue`
改写点：carry most of the volume→leans on；handbags and a few watch models→a narrow handful of product categories；a change in taste is a change in revenue→the whole business（的脆弱性）。
