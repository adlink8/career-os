## Part I / Writing / (30 minutes)

**Directions:** _Suppose your classmate Chen Yu has just won the first prize in the national college students' robotics competition, and that you were among the few who saw how long it took her to get there. You are to write her a letter to congratulate her on the award, recall two specific things you noticed about the way she prepared, and invite her to share her experience with your class. You will have 30 minutes for the task. You should write at least <u>120</u> words but no more than <u>180</u> words._

写作提示（半开放情景，卷面只给方向、不给细节）：
- 已知：陈宇是你同班同学，刚在全国大学生机器人大赛获一等奖；你目睹过她备赛。
- 你需要自拟：两件**可察可核**的备赛细节（如两个暑假留校、同一部件重做若干版、厚厚的实验记录本、赛前夜仍在调参数、把失败原因讲给队友听）、这些细节说明的品质（耐心、抗挫、愿意分享）、分享活动的时间与形式（班会/线上问答、时长、主题）、请对方回复的截止日。

### 满分范文（实测 169 词，含称呼与落款；正文 162 词，均在 120–180 区间内）

Dear Chen Yu,

**Congratulations on your first prize at the national robotics competition!** Half our class heard the news before lunch.

Anyone can congratulate a winner; few of us saw the rest. **While most of us went home for two summers, you stayed in the workshop rebuilding one gripper after another, and your logbook is now thicker than any textbook we own.** **What impressed me most was the way you explained each failed trial to your teammates so that nobody had to repeat your mistakes.** The award reached your bench for good reason.

**May I ask a favor on behalf of the class? Our class meeting on October 12 has thirty free minutes, and we would be glad if you used them to walk us through your preparation: weekly targets, and the night before a deadline.** **Would a face-to-face talk suit you better, or a short online question session?** Just let me know by Friday.

Once again, congratulations, and we are proud of you.

Yours sincerely,
Li Ming

<summary>Passage One 原文（Tapescript）</summary>

In 1892 a Scottish chemist working in London built a bottle that kept boiling water hot for a day and liquid hydrogen cold for hours, with no fuel and no packing at all. James Dewar, a lecturer at the Royal Institution, needed gases in liquid form while he examined them, so he fitted one thin glass tube inside another, silvered the surfaces that faced each other, and drew the air out of the space between them. With no air left, heat could travel neither by touching nor by moving current, and the bright walls threw back whatever warmth arrived by radiation. Dewar asked for no legal rights over what he had made, and colleagues remembered a man uninterested in money. That silence opened a door. Two Berlin glassblowers who had watched his demonstrations filed papers of their own in 1904 and sold the rights to a factory in Hanover, which shipped a household version under the name Thermos. For six decades that was a brand. In 1963 an American court agreed with a rival maker that the public had long used the word for any such bottle, so it slipped out of protection and into the dictionary, and the German firm kept none of it. The design still holds a place in laboratories: a steel vacuum vessel carries liquid nitrogen, medical samples and vaccines across countries with unreliable power, and every cheap flask on a camping shelf is his, though almost nobody buying it has heard his name.
