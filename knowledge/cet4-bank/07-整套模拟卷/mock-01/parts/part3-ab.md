**The train gains ground**

**A)** On a July evening in 2023 a 22-year-old Dutch student posted a photograph that captured a change most transport experts had stopped expecting: four months, four countries, zero flights. Her rail itinerary from Utrecht to the Croatian coast was shared tens of thousands of times. A generation that had happily flown anywhere for forty euros had discovered the couchette. Surveys find that Europeans under thirty are roughly twice as likely as their parents to choose the train for a mid-distance trip, even when the plane leaves earlier and costs less. What began as thrift and nostalgia is hardening into conviction: the way you travel now says what you believe.

**B)** The numbers back the mood. Eurostar carried a record 18.6 million passengers through the Channel tunnel in 2023, surpassing its pre-pandemic best, while state railways from Austria to Sweden reported their busiest years on record. Night trains returned route by route — Berlin to Paris, Vienna to Rome — many sold out weeks in advance. Germany, a country of cheap flights and notorious punctuality problems, watched regional ridership climb by double digits one summer after it introduced a flat-rate monthly ticket. Pressed by analysts about the trend, airline executives chose words that sounded uncomfortably like concern.

**C)** The first reason trains are winning is arithmetic, not ideology. A seventy-minute flight between Amsterdam and Frankfurt routinely consumes six hours of a traveler's day once airport trains, security queues, boarding and baggage claim are counted; a plane is fast only between runways. Rail stations, by contrast, sit inside the cities themselves; passengers walk aboard with a backpack and no check-in, and a train running at 300 kilometers an hour can place a traveler in a neighboring capital sooner than a flight twice as distant. Where the clock, not the fare, decides the choice, rail now often wins on routes well short of a thousand kilometers.

**D)** The second reason is carbon. On almost any measure an electric train produces a small fraction of the greenhouse gases of a short flight: European Environment Agency research put high-speed rail near fourteen grams of CO2 per passenger-kilometer against roughly 250 for short-haul aviation, a difference of about twenty times. The gap survives even a coal-heavy electricity grid, and it widens with occupancy, since one crowded carriage replaces dozens of cars as well as a half-empty jet whose dirtiest minutes are its takeoff and climb.

**E)** Governments have drawn their own conclusions. In 2021 France's parliament voted to prohibit domestic flights on routes where a train could make the trip in under two and a half hours; by 2023 the first three regional air links had been withdrawn, with more promised once rail connections improved. Vienna had moved earlier and more quietly. Austria's 2020 rescue of its flag airline came with a pledge that no destination reachable by rail inside the same time limit would ever be served by air again, and the morning shuttle between Vienna and Salzburg was dropped that year to loud applause from environmental groups.

**F)** The airlines, meanwhile, are adapting rather than dying. Facing higher ticket taxes, scarce slots and hopeless margins on segments below 500 kilometers, Europe's majors are quietly pruning their shortest legs, retiring fifty-seat jets and sending crews toward longer, richer routes. Air France has promised deep cuts in domestic departures in exchange for state support; Lufthansa has grounded entire classes of small aircraft. The hub system runs in reverse: instead of planes feeding planes, trains now feed the hub, and carriers have accepted a pecking order that would have insulted them a decade ago.

**G)** The train, however, is no automatic champion. Cross-border travel remains an embarrassment: each national operator sells through its own aging website, fares are assembled from separately priced national legs, and one ticket across three countries — a purchase an airline customer completes in seconds — can take half an hour and still cost several times a budget flight. New lines need decades and mountains of money, while the capacity that exists is choked by freight, by restored border checks and by signaling that stops politely at every frontier.

**H)** The comparison itself is tilted by policy. International aviation buys tax-free jet fuel, and tickets usually escape value-added tax on departing international flights — privileges nearly as old as the industry. Rail, by contrast, pays electricity levies, track access charges and station fees at every border it crosses. A traveler may therefore see a 19-euro flight to a city three train-hours away next to a 90-euro rail fare inclusive of every tax. What appears on the screen, in other words, does not measure what either mode truly costs the planet or the public purse.

**I)** History suggests what happens when travelers count real totals. Spain's first high-speed line, linking Madrid and Seville across 390 kilometers in about two and a half hours since 1992, hollowed out the parallel air route; the last direct flights between those cities were withdrawn in 2019 after decades of bleeding passengers to the rails. Between Madrid and Barcelona, trains now carry something like four times the riders of planes on the same city pair. Wherever Europe has built dense, fast and well-connected rail, the pattern repeats; nowhere else does it.

**J)** The limits are as real as the revival. Rail cannot serve an island, a mountain village or a low-density suburb without a station, and it cannot cross oceans at all. Beyond roughly a thousand kilometers the plane reclaims the day: a five-hour train is a lifestyle, not a commute, and meetings in two time zones will not be rescheduled for anyone. Even rail's most enthusiastic defenders concede that aviation must keep the long and the thin routes, while cargo moves by air in volumes no freight train can touch.

**K)** What comes next will be settled less by engineering than by administration. The European Union's old ambition of putting every major city within three hours of high-speed rail by 2030 is quietly moving into the archive of unfinished promises; the realistic progress is smaller — joint booking platforms, through-tickets that honor cross-border delays, night paths for freight off saturated lines. If those pieces click together, the future of European travel will be neither trains nor planes alone but a negotiated settlement between them. The airplane's century of unchallenged dominance has ended; the argument over the arrangement replacing it has barely begun.

#### Section A 选词填空（Q26–35）

**词库词性分类**
- 名词（4）：A) belonging　B) credit　M) reward　O) transaction
- 形容词（4）：C) economical　G) mutual　J) remarkable　L) remote
- 副词（3）：I) particularly　K) elsewhere　N) significantly
- 动词（4）：D) emerged　E) linked　F) merged　H) notice
- 答案 10 题（名3/形2/副2/动3）；干扰 5 词：C) economical、F) merged、G) mutual、K) elsewhere、M) reward

**结构说明（本版修订要点）**：原词库 particularly / remarkably / significantly 三个 -ly 副词与 32（副词+比较级）、33（副词+原级形容词）两槽同型互抢，构成双解隐患。现将 K) remarkably 换为地点副词 elsewhere，词库内**可修饰比较级的程度副词只剩 significantly，可修饰原级形容词的只剩 particularly**，两槽各自唯一可判。另将 27 空后句改为 "saved and spent later"，用"入账后可储蓄、可消费"的 credit 专属搭配排除 reward。

**26. E) linked**　语法依据：`had been ___ to each other through a local time bank`，had been 后需及物动词过去分词构成被动，且框架 be linked to each other through a scheme 唯一。干扰 F) merged（merge 表"合为一体"，搭配 with/into 不接 to each other，两人亦不可能"融合成一个"）；D) emerged（不及物，无被动）；H) notice（原形，不能跟在 had been 后）。

**27. B) credit**　语法依据：`earns a ___ that can be saved and spent later`，冠词 a 后需可数名词单数。判定证据：save and spend 是货币/记账额度（credit）的专属搭配链——credit 先"存"后"花"，正对应时间银行"存时间、花时间"机制。干扰 M) reward（一次性报酬，只能 receive/collect，不能被 save up 并当作额度 spend 出去，不可循环）；O) transaction（"saved and spent"语义不可能）；A) belonging（不可数，a 后不立）。

**28. D) emerged**　语法依据：`The idea ___ in Japan in the 1970s`，in the 1970s 要求一般过去时主动词，其后无宾语（不及物），主语为单一抽象概念"起源"义。干扰 F) merged（"融合"需两个以上对象，主语 the idea 单一）；E) linked（需宾语或 to/with 补足语）；H) notice（主语须有生命，且"想法注意到"不通）。

**29. J) remarkable**　语法依据：`What is ___ about the modern version is its software: ...`，What is + 表语形容词，评价"现代版本值得注意之处"，冒号后接亮点解释，与段首 "nothing new" 构成"旧主意、新亮点"对照。干扰 C) economical（要求成本/价格语境，冒号后陈述的是功能亮点而非省钱）；G) mutual（"相互"需成对体验者，不能作此表语）；I) particularly / K) elsewhere / N) significantly（副词不能置于 is 之后作表语）。

**30. O) transaction**　语法依据：`an act of neighborliness than a commercial ___`，commercial 后需可数名词单数；"邻里互助 vs 商业交易"是固定对立框架。干扰 B) credit（"商业信贷"不与"邻里行为"构成交换方式层面的对照，且 credit 已被 27 的 save-and-spent 框架锁定）。

**31. A) belonging**　语法依据：`a fresh sense of ___`，介词 of 后需名词；sense of belonging（归属感）为固定表达，同位语 "of being part of a place rather than merely passing through it" 进一步把词义钉死在"归属"上。干扰 M) reward（"a sense of reward"虽可说，但与同位语"成为地方的一部分"矛盾）；C) economical / G) mutual / J) remarkable / L) remote（形容词，词性不合）。

**32. N) significantly**　语法依据：`reported ___ higher levels of trust ... than`，副词修饰比较级 higher。程度副词中唯有 significantly 可修饰比较级（significantly higher 为研究数据表述的固定搭配）。干扰 I) particularly（不能修饰比较级，particularly higher 不合法）；K) elsewhere（地点副词，不能修饰形容词比较级）。

**33. I) particularly**　语法依据：`still ___ hard to find`，副词修饰原级形容词 hard。词库三个副词中，N) significantly 要求可量化的差异/变化（✗ significantly hard），K) elsewhere 为地点副词（✗），唯有 particularly hard 成立。

**34. L) remote**　语法依据：`members in ___ areas`，名词 areas 前需定语形容词，且下文 "spend so long traveling" 要求"偏远"义——remote areas 为固定搭配。干扰 C) economical（"经济实惠的"不修饰地理区域）；G) mutual（"相互的"不可修饰 areas）；J) remarkable（语法可立但与"路上花很长时间"无因果联系）。

**35. H) notice**　语法依据：`will ___ if I don't appear`，will 后接动词原形且其后无宾语。词库其余动词 D/E/F 均为过去式/过去分词形式，不能置于 will 后，词性层面唯一。语义与前文"整天不说话"对照，指"有人会发觉我不出现"。
