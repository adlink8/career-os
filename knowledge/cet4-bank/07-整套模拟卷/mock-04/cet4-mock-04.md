### 满分范文（实测正文 160 词，含称呼与落款 167 词，均在 120–180 区间内）

Dear Ms. Zhou,

I am writing to thank you for everything you did for me during my first year.

When I joined the Volunteer Mentoring Program, I could not find a lecture hall without a campus map. **You never handed me answers; you sat with me until I learned to plan my own week.** **That habit has kept me on time, and calmer, ever since.**

**What I remember best is the evening I failed a calculus quiz and went to your office to ask about dropping the course.** Instead, **you told me about your own failed attempts, then walked me through every problem I had got wrong until I could explain them back to you.** My grade in that course is the lowest I have ever earned and the one I am proudest of.

Next term I will apply to be a mentor myself, **so that a nervous freshman can have someone waiting at the bus stop.** Thank you again for your patience.

Yours sincerely,
Li Ming

（亮点句型说明：① You never handed me answers; you sat with me until… ——分号并置的对比句 + until 时间状语从句，用"没有替我做什么/却陪我做什么"界定帮助的性质，是感谢信避免空喊 thanks 的关键写法；② That habit has kept me on time, and calmer, ever since——现在完成时 + keep sb + 形容词的复合宾语，插入语 and calmer 补出心态变化，对应"影响"这一任务点；③ What I remember best is the evening I failed… ——what 引导的名词性从句作主语 + 省略关系词的定语从句修饰 the evening，用具体场景代替抽象评价，属高分开头手法；④ walk sb through sth（逐步讲解）为地道动词短语，until I could explain them back to you 用"能讲回去"证明真懂，比 "I improved a lot" 可信；⑤ the lowest I have ever earned and the one I am proudest of ——最高级 + the one 并列的悖论式收束，制造情感张力；⑥ so that a nervous freshman can have someone waiting at the bus stop——so that 目的状语从句 + have sb doing，把"回报"落在具体画面而非口号上，与首段的"找不到教室"形成闭环。全篇美式拼写与用词：program、freshman、calculus，无 -ise/behaviour 类英式形。）

<summary>Passage One 原文（Tapescript）</summary>

Six raised dots have carried more reading than most printing presses ever did. The system introduced by Louis Braille in France in the 1820s took twelve positions from a military night-writing code and cut them to six, a number a fingertip can take in without moving at all. That choice is the reason the code still works: a trained adult's finger crosses a cell in well under a second and sustains about one hundred and thirty words a minute, not far behind a sighted reader of the same text. For a century the obstacle was supply rather than design. Embossing a book needed a special press, expensive paper, and proofreaders who could check the raised page by touch, so most schoolbooks simply never appeared, and through the twentieth century the share of blind children in wealthy countries who learned the code slid from a clear majority to roughly one in ten. Audio appeared to fill the hole, but it did not. Comparisons of matched groups found that people who take material in through their ears spell worse and handle long sentences less confidently than people who follow the same passage with a finger, because a spoken stream rarely shows where one word stops and the next begins. Displays that raise and lower pins can now present a page of written text arriving from a telephone. The barrier that remains is price: a serviceable unit costs about as much as a used car.

<summary>Passage Two 原文（Tapescript）</summary>

Over a hundred meters the margin between a final and a medal is often a tenth of a second, and a large part of that tenth is spent before the runner has moved. Track's governing body disqualifies anyone who leaves the blocks sooner than one hundred milliseconds after the gun, a rule built on laboratory work showing that no human being can hear a sound, decide what it means, and contract a leg muscle inside that window; an athlete faster than the limit was guessing. Within the legal range, elite starters respond at roughly one tenth to one sixth of a second, while untrained runners sit nearer four tenths. Yet a study of national-level sprinters found that the size of that delay explained almost none of the variation in how quickly they covered the first ten meters. What mattered was how hard and how abruptly an athlete drove the blocks backward: the quickest starters produced large forces in a low, forward direction within two hundred milliseconds of the gun, and their arms began to swing before the legs had finished straightening. The same researchers noted that waiting for a single sound is cheaper for the nervous system than choosing among several, which is why a stadium that goes quiet before the gun helps every lane and why one spectator's shutter can cost a finalist her season. Coaches now train the push rather than the ear.

<summary>Passage Three 原文（Tapescript）</summary>

Every glacier map begins with a number nobody can see: the thickness of the ice far from any margin, where no surveyor can stand. For a century the answer came from bamboo poles pushed down through a borehole, from dynamite soundings on a frozen lake, or from a traveler's guess at the depth of the valley, and each method left blank spaces on the sheet. Radar changed that. A pulse sent from an aircraft or a small unmanned craft bounces off the rock underneath, and the time it takes to return, divided by the speed of the wave through ice, gives a reading every few meters along the flight line. Satellites add the height of the surface, one instrument using a laser and another microwaves, and combining the two tells researchers how much of a flow is floating and how fast its top is sinking. The newest approach pairs radar images taken days apart and detects motion of a fraction of a centimeter in a day, enough to warn a village beneath a surging tongue weeks before it arrives. None of this is easy. Debris-covered lobes in the Himalaya return a weak signal and hide real losses, meltwater ponds bend both height and velocity readings, and a glacier that stores snow for a decade may spend it in one warm season. Even so, the merged record now reaches most of the planet's ice and feeds forecasts of how long rivers born of summer melt will keep running.

### 参考译文

In recent years, to help older residents who live at home with the daily difficulty of cooking and buying their meals, many cities have responded by building community canteens that provide affordable and convenient meal assistance for the elderly. Most of them are set up inside residential neighborhoods, where the dishes are low in oil and salt and cooked soft enough for older people, and customers over seventy can get a discount with a meal card. For the oldest residents, who have trouble getting around, communities organize volunteers and delivery riders to bring hot meals to their doors. To make up for the losses in the first months of operation, some canteens open to the general public during the day and use the income from outside diners to subsidize the meal service. Today, an ordinary lunch has joined day care and doctor visits at home as a basic part of community care for the elderly. Many older residents say that the canteen, with its familiar faces and laughter, is far livelier than eating alone in front of the television.

### 得分点

1. **"近年来" + 句首不定式目的状语 + 现在完成时**（In recent years, to help older residents who live at home with the daily difficulty of…, many cities have responded by building…）：本段主时态为现在完成时，全篇误用一般过去时累计扣分；"为化解……难题"用 to help sb with the difficulty of doing / to solve the problem 前置表目的，比硬起一句 In order that 更简洁；"把建设社区食堂作为应对之策"respond by building / turn to community canteens as an answer，写 take … as a measure 亦可；"做饭难、吃饭难"是民生政策语汇，意译 the daily difficulty of cooking and buying their meals 给分，逐字直译 cook difficult, eat difficult 不得分。
2. **"为老年人提供……"定语从句 + provide 搭配**（community canteens that provide affordable and convenient meal assistance for the elderly）：先行词 canteens + that 定语从句是本主题的核心句式；provide sb with sth / provide sth for sb 两种搭配均可，"实惠便捷"affordable and convenient；"助餐服务"按卷面提示写 meal assistance service，写 meal aid / dining help 酌情给分；"居家老人"older residents who live at home / the elderly living at home，直译 home old people 不得分。
3. **"饭菜少油少盐、软硬适中" + where 非限定定语从句**（…, where the dishes are low in oil and salt and cooked soft enough for older people）：where 引导的非限定定语从句把"开在居民区内"与食堂内部描写并成一句，是拉开档次的衔接手段；"少油少盐"be low in oil and salt（be 动词不可省），"软硬适中"cooked soft enough / neither too hard nor too greasy，写 food is soft 属漏译"适中"。
4. **"七十岁以上的老人持卡就餐还可享受折扣"**（customers over seventy can get a discount with a meal card）：over seventy 为介词短语后置定语，"持卡"用 with a meal card 表方式，二者构成本句的给分点组合；get a discount / enjoy a discount / get a discounted price 均可给分；"还可"译出 can / even 即可，漏译不扣分，把"持卡"误译为 hold the card and eat 属汉式直译扣分。
5. **"行动不便的高龄老人" + 非限定定语从句 + 地道动词**（For the oldest residents, who have trouble getting around, communities organize volunteers and delivery riders to bring hot meals to their doors）：who have trouble getting around / have mobility problems 是"行动不便"的标准译法，直译 walk inconveniently 不得分；organize sb to do sth；"送到家中"写 bring sth to sb's door / deliver meals to their homes 均属地道转达，"外卖骑手"delivery riders / food delivery workers。
6. **"为解决开业初期的亏损……用……补贴……"双重目的表达**（To make up for the losses in the first months of operation, some canteens open to the general public during the day and use the income from outside diners to subsidize the meal service）：句首不定式与句末 use sth to do 形成"目的—手段"闭环，是本卷最长也最易失分的句子；"亏损"losses / running at a loss，"向社会顾客开放"open to the general public / outside customers，"补贴"subsidize / offset the cost of（美式拼写 -ize）。
7. **"已与……一起被列为……基本内容" 现在完成时 + as 补足语 + 三项并列**（an ordinary lunch has joined day care and doctor visits at home as a basic part of community care for the elderly）：主语是"一顿寻常的午饭"，谓语三单且必须用现在完成时；has joined A and B as… / has been listed together with A and B as… 两种句式均可给分（后者为被动写法），写成 has been listed 而漏掉 as 引导的补足成分扣结构分；"日间照料"按卷面提示写 day care，"上门看病"doctor visits at home / home medical visits；"社区养老服务"community care for the elderly / community elderly-care services。
8. **"比一个人守着电视吃饭热闹得多" 比较级 + 动名词比较对象**（the canteen, with its familiar faces and laughter, is far livelier than eating alone in front of the television）：比较项必须对称（livelier than eating…，不可写成 than we eat alone）；"热闹得多"用 far / much livelier 表程度，more lively 为拼写错误；with its familiar faces and laughter 作插入伴随状语对应"有熟人、有说笑声"。本卷热点词汇：养老 elderly care、居民区 residential neighborhood（美式拼写，非 neighbourhood）、志愿者 volunteer；专有名词按卷面提示照抄，不必自造译名。
