**The tabletop revival**

**A)** On a wet Tuesday in Antwerp, ninety people who barely know one another are arguing, politely, about wheat prices in eighteenth-century Saxony. Nobody at the twenty tables is checking a phone. This is a hobby that refused to die on schedule: since the mid-2010s, retail spending on boxed tabletop games has climbed in nearly every year in Europe and North America while almost every other toy category shrank, and the price of an ordinary release has roughly tripled since the 1990s. Designers and shop owners still hear the same objection, delivered as though the case were closed — who pays real money for cardboard?

**B)** The answer is an industry rather than a club. Analysts put worldwide retail sales of tabletop games above twelve billion dollars a year, and that figure doubles if trading cards are counted. More revealing is where the money to publish comes from. Between 2012 and 2020 the largest source of new titles was not a company's budget but a crowd: thousands of projects collected their printing costs from future customers on crowdfunding sites, and one strategy game raised nearly four million dollars in a month. Those buyers also said what they wanted before a press was switched on, a better forecast than any a marketing department can buy.

**C)** Ask regulars why they come back and the answer is rarely about the components. A modern game is built for conversation in a way that solitary screen play is not: the rulebook supplies a subject, the turns supply a rhythm, and six strangers can spend three hours debating sheep without discussing anybody's job. Psychologists have a term for the relief — joint attention, the comfort of sharing a focus so that talking stops being a performance. Nobody at the table has to be interesting.

**D)** The second change happened inside the boxes. Older titles eliminated players early and then asked them to watch; sitting for an hour while other people take turns is now treated as poor manners. German designers of eurogames built systems that keep everybody working until the final tally, copied one another openly, and signed their names to the rulebook, turning a disposable commodity into an authored object. Cooperation arrived soon after: if the whole table can lose to the box, nobody has to be beaten, and a friendly evening stops depending on hurt feelings.

**E)** Every improvement made the product heavier. A typical release ships with a molded insert, linen-finish cards, wooden blocks and a board thick enough to prop open a door, and because carriers bill by volume and mass rather than by what a parcel is worth, the scale at the depot decides most of a box's real cost. Almost all of it is printed in and around Guangzhou, where one plant can run ten thousand copies in a week and where a minimum order can keep an added set out of print for years. When container rates quadrupled in 2021, the cheapest half of a game became the dearest.

**F)** The shops that sell these objects survived by becoming what a supermarket shelf cannot be. A game is hard to buy on impulse and easy to love after one round, so a profitable independent store runs demo nights, keeps a back room of tables, and pays for that floor space with hope more than rent. Its economics are lopsided: the hot titles that pull a crowd through the door carry so thin a markup that a shop is glad to sell them at cost, while the money lives in the oddments on the next shelf, small printings that no chain warehouse will touch.

**G)** The screen, meanwhile, turned out to be a window rather than a wall. Free or nearly free electronic adaptations of a tabletop game can be learned in a browser on a lunch break, and they teach a rule system in a way no box description manages. Players who master a classic on a game platform often buy the physical version afterward for the table, not the monitor. Far from finishing paper games, software became the cheapest tutor and the most patient salesman the trade has ever had.

**H)** Institutions have noticed. School districts in several countries purchase strategy boxes alongside microscopes, after classrooms that played a game about scarce resources for eight weeks measured better on fraction tests and planning tasks; speech therapists run party games to practice taking turns; stroke wards order cooperative titles because losing together asks nothing of a patient's pride. Nursing homes found that a resident who cannot recall breakfast may still play a hand of cards correctly. In every case the purchase is justified by an outcome that has no bearing on amusement.

**I)** Growth this fast carries its own punishment. During the pandemic, printers, designers and shops took orders as though the surge would never end; warehouses filled, and in 2022 a backlog of unsold copies was cleared by discount chains in bargain bins, which taught a new generation of customers to wait. Freight rates fell, but so did spending, while licensing fees and paper quotes climbed together. A trade that had supported thousands of illustrators, testers and event staff through the boom shed much of that work in the correction, and several stores closed within a single season.

**J)** Researchers who study play warn that none of this is automatic. In one experiment, groups that spent an evening on a competitive game enjoyed themselves more but recalled less about one another than groups given a cooperative title; in another, pairs playing with a smartphone face-up on the table talked for barely half as long as pairs with no phone in sight. Presence, such work suggests, has to be arranged for. A stack of cardboard guarantees nothing on its own, and cafés that allow laptops at the weekend are often surprised by what they serve.

**K)** What the next decade is made of is still open. Libraries and second-hand channels have become the main route by which older designs survive, and a resold box stays in use long after its first owner moves on. Print-on-demand now makes an edition of two hundred possible, though the unit price still punishes anything unusual. Designers prototype with shared files and test by video link, while plastic miniatures — the dearest and most wasteful part of a modern box — are increasingly swapped for wood and card, which cost less to fly and outlast whatever shelf they land on.

#### Section A 选词填空（Q26–35）

**词库词性分类**

- 形容词（4）：D) ambient　K) moderate　L) modest　M) porous
- 副词（4）：E) barely　H) legally　J) merely　N) steadily
- 动词（4）：A) absorb　B) adjusted　C) adopted　O) tackling

**结构说明（唯一性设计）**：26 与 29、31 三处动词槽分别锁定"过去式+to""情态动词后原形""句首现在分词状语"三种互斥形态，C) adopted 只在形态上可疑、在框架（不接 to、无被动语义主语）上必排；三处形容词槽位各不相同（27 冠词+名词、28 冠词+数词、35 名词前定语），K) moderate 与 L) modest 的同形干扰由"听起来近乎道歉的数字"这一"低于预期"语义强制排除；副词槽 32 需修饰名词性表语、33 需修饰动词且与 year after year 构成"渐进"义，E) barely / J) merely 虽可修饰名词或数量，但在 not only…but 的递进框架中语义自相矛盾。名词槽 30 由 the public ___ on the corner 的"临街公共文化建筑"义唯一确定。词库 15 词的原形（含 absorb/adopt/moderate/library/burden/porous/ambient/tackle/adjust/legal/merely/barely/steady/laboratory 各词形）均未在短文中以任何形式提前出现。

**26. B) adjusted**　语法依据：`something residents simply ___ to by shutting a window`，先行词 something 是介词 to 的宾语，从句需一个可接 to 的及物动词过去式（一般过去时描述过去的常态），adjust to sth（适应）为固定搭配。干扰 C) adopted（及物，直接接宾语，不接 to；"被居民采纳到"语义不立）；A) absorb（原形，且吸收的应是噪音本身而非"天气"）；O) tackling（分词作从句谓语不合，且缺宾语）。

**27. M) porous**　语法依据：`a ___ asphalt whose surface fills with tiny pockets`，冠词与名词之间需定语形容词，定语从句"表面充满小孔"给出定义式限定，porous（多孔的）与 pockets 构成同义互证。干扰 D) ambient（"周围环境的"只修饰温度、噪声、照度等环境量，不修饰材料）；K) moderate / L) modest（"适度的、不大的"不能引出"满是气孔"的物理解释）；E) barely / J) merely / N) steadily（副词不能置于 a 与名词之间）。

**28. L) modest**　语法依据：`removed a ___ four decibels, a figure that sounds almost apologetic`，"a+形容词+数词"结构表"区区四个分贝"；同位语 almost apologetic（近乎道歉）与下文"却区分了嘈杂与可忍"构成先抑后扬，要求词义为"小得不好意思说出口"。干扰 K) moderate（"中等偏温和、不过分"，不含"低于预期"义，与 apologetic 的自贬语气冲突，且 a moderate four decibels 不成搭配）；D) ambient（不修饰数量）；E) barely（副词，位置不合法）。

**29. A) absorb**　语法依据：`panels of recycled rubber can now ___ a further twelve decibels`，情态动词 can 后需动词原形且后接宾语（分贝数），主语为吸声板材，absorb（吸收声音）为声学固定搭配。干扰 B) adjusted / C) adopted（过去式形态不能置于 can 后）；O) tackling（分词，不能作 can 后谓语）。

**30. I) library**　语法依据：`as hushed as the public ___ on the corner`，比较结构 as hushed as 要求填入以"安静"为制度性特征的对象，the public library on the corner（街角的公共图书馆）是英语中"临街公共文化建筑"的固定意象，且与"楼上公寓"同属日常生活空间。干扰 G) laboratory（实验室属门禁式科研场所，不以"公共""临街"存在，也不是居家安静的文化基准）；F) burden（不可数语义，且"像负担一样安静"不成立）。

**31. O) tackling**　语法依据：句首 `___ noise at the tire and the wall, councils now attack it by district`，逗号后为主句，需现在分词短语作伴随/方式状语，其逻辑主语 councils 与"在源头处理噪音"构成主动关系，且与主句 attack it by district 形成"从材料到分区"的并列推进。干扰 B) adjusted / C) adopted（过去分词置于句首表被动，"议会被调整/被采纳"语义不立）；A) absorb（原形不能引导状语）。

**32. H) legally**　语法依据：`not only rude but ___ an offense`，not only…but (also) 递进框架要求填入使语气加重的成分，副词修饰名词性表语 an offense，legally an offense（在法律上构成违规）与前文 councils writing rules 呼应。干扰 J) merely / E) barely（两者均表"只不过、几乎不算"，是弱化，与 but 引导的递进方向自相矛盾）；N) steadily（"稳定地"不能修饰定性判断）。

**33. N) steadily**　语法依据：`complaints have fallen ___, year after year, with no new equipment installed`，现在完成时+持续多年的语境需一个表"渐进稳定"的方式副词修饰 fallen，year after year 是其唯一同义指向。干扰 E) barely（"几乎未降"与 have fallen 直接矛盾）；J) merely（"仅仅下降"需后接限定对象，此处无）。

**34. F) burden**　语法依据：`more than a million healthy years of life lost ... , a ___ paid in sleep and diagnosed ... decades later`，同位语前需可数名词单数，指代前文的疾病负担；burden（负担、负重）与 paid、diagnosed 构成"由健康承担的代价"搭配，burden of disease 为公共卫生固定术语。干扰 I) library / G) laboratory（语义不可能"在睡眠中偿付"）；D) ambient / K) moderate / L) modest（形容词不能作 a 后的中心名词）。

**35. D) ambient**　语法依据：`As ___ levels creep upward with every added lane`，名词 levels 前需定语形容词，ambient levels（环境本底水平）是噪声监测的标准术语，与"每加一条车道就上升"的环境累积义吻合。干扰 L) modest / K) moderate（"适中的水平逐年上升"逻辑自相抵消，且无法与 creeping upward 构成术语链）；M) porous（材料属性，不修饰 levels）。
