## 📖 Reading Passage: The Sunk Cost Fallacy: Why We Cling to Failing Endeavors (沉没成本谬误：非理性执迷的心理学解构)

Whether it is sitting through a dreadful two-hour motion picture simply because one purchased an expensive ticket, or continuing to pour millions of dollars into an obsolete corporate software initiative, human decision-makers frequently fall victim to a classic cognitive distortion: the sunk cost fallacy. In classical economic theory, rational economic actors are presumed to make forward-looking choices based strictly on marginal future costs and expected future benefits. Prior expenditures that cannot be recovered—known as sunk costs—should theoretically exert zero influence on subsequent choices. Yet, in practice, human psychology stubbornly defies standard economic orthodoxy.

The cognitive mechanism driving this irrational persistence is intimately tied to what Nobel laureates Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky identified as 'loss aversion.' To the human brain, the psychological pain of experiencing a loss is roughly twice as potent as the pleasure derived from an equivalent gain. When an individual abandons a floundering project, they are forced to formally acknowledge that their past investment of time, money, or personal reputation was utterly wasted. To evade this painful psychological reckoning, people instinctively escalate their commitment, irrationally convincing themselves that just a little more effort or capital will miraculously reverse their fortunes.

Behavioral economists have demonstrated that this bias is compounded by emotional ego-defense mechanisms. In a landmark field experiment involving enterprise managers, researchers observed how participants reacted when given the option to terminate underperforming commercial projects. Managers who had originally proposed and championed the project invested substantially more additional capital into the failing venture than newly appointed outside managers who inherited the exact same data. The original champions were blinded by a desperate compulsion to justify their initial judgment, confusing objective commercial viability with personal validation.

Overcoming the sunk cost fallacy requires cultivating dispassionate decision hygiene. Effective organizations construct institutional guardrails—such as pre-established milestone exit criteria, external auditing committees, and explicit incentives for acknowledging when an initiative has reached a dead end. Furthermore, leaders must cultivate a culture where terminating a failing project is framed not as an ignominious failure, but as a strategic reallocation of scarce resources toward higher-yielding opportunities. True wisdom lies not in dogged, blind perseverance, but in possessing the clarity to walk away when the path forward leads nowhere.

### 🏮 全文逐段地道精翻

无论是因为买了一张昂贵的电影票而硬着头皮看完一场奇烂无比的两小时电影，还是继续向一个早已落伍的企业软件项目砸入数百万资金，人类决策者常常沦为一种经典认知偏差的受害者：沉没成本谬误。在经典经济学理论中，理性的经济人被假定纯粹基于未来的边际成本和预期收益来做出前瞻性选择。过往那些已无法挽回的开销——即所谓的沉没成本——在理论上对后续决策的影响应当为零。然而在现实中，人类心理顽固地背离了标准经济学的正统教条。

驱动这种非理性执迷的认知机制，与诺贝尔奖得主丹尼尔·卡尼曼和阿莫斯·特沃斯基发现的“损失厌恶”密切相关。对于人类大脑而言，遭受损失所带来的心理剧痛，大约是获得同等收益所带来欣悦感的两倍。当一个人选择终止一个摇摇欲坠的项目时，他被迫在形式上正式承认：自己过往投入的时间、金钱或个人声誉被彻头彻尾地浪费了。为了逃避这种撕心裂肺的心理清算，人们本能地选择“追加承诺”，非理性地自欺欺人，坚信只要再加一把劲或多投点资金，就能奇迹般扭转败局。

行为经济学家证实，这种偏见还会被人类自尊心的心理防御机制进一步放大。在一项针对企业高管的里程碑式实证研究中，研究人员观察了受试者在获得终止不良项目权限时的真实反应。那些最初发起并极力主推该项目的高管，向这一注定失败的项目中追加了数倍的资金，远超那些拿到完全相同数据、中途接管的新任外部经理人。最初的发起者被急于证明自己当初英明判断的绝望执念蒙蔽了双眼，将客观的商业可行性与虚妄的个人自尊证明混为一谈。

战胜沉没成本谬误，需要我们在决策中建立冷峻客观的“心智卫生习惯”。卓越的组织往往会构筑制度化的防火墙——例如预设关键节点的退场红线、设立独立的外部审计评估委员会，并对勇于承认项目走到死胡同的行为给予明确制度激励。更重要的是，领袖必须在组织内部培育这样一种文化：终止失败的项目绝非丢人现眼的屈辱溃败，而是将稀缺资源战略性抽离并重新配置到高回报赛道的英明抉择。真正的智慧从来不是盲目蛮干的死磕，而是在明知前路无望时具备从容抽身而退的决断力。
